WEEK 19 SUMMARY — Does Increased Complexity Reduce Overfitting? (extends Week 18)

================================================================
MOTIVATION (Professor Schafer)
================================================================
Week 18 tested two regularization routes against the baseline's overfitting
gap: a simpler (16,8) architecture, and a dropout sweep (p=0.1/0.2/0.3) on the
paper's standard 32->16->8 architecture. The professor's stated next step was
to extend to INCREASED model complexity paired with dropout (p=0.4), to test
the opposite direction: does giving the network more capacity, combined with
dropout, close the in-sample/out-of-sample gap further than Week 18's routes
did -- or does added capacity just overfit harder unless dropout compensates?

================================================================
DESIGN
================================================================
One architecture, much bigger than Week 18's baseline: hidden_sizes
[128, 64, 32, 16] (vs the paper's 32->16->8), swept at dropout
p = 0.0 / 0.02 / 0.4:
  p=0.0  -- "complex, no dropout" anchor (isolates the effect of capacity alone)
  p=0.02 -- light dropout
  p=0.4  -- heavy dropout (the professor's suggested extension point)
Five-day-ahead horizon only (same scope as Week 18's dropout task). All six
Jiang frameworks x mode combinations covered (BS+NN, Heston+NN independent/
sequential, HestonFeature and Pure NN sequential).

================================================================
HEADLINE — CROSS-WEEK COMPARISON (Cells 19.4-19.5)
================================================================
The actual research question, answered directly:

1. Complexity ALONE (p=0.0, no dropout) made overfitting WORSE, not better,
   in all 6 configs -- overfit ratio rose by +35.0 pp on average vs the Week 18
   baseline (e.g. HestonFeature Seq: 279% -> 415%; Heston+NN Seq: 55% -> 83%).
   A bigger network with no regularization simply has more capacity to
   memorize the training set. This refutes the naive "more complexity helps"
   hypothesis on its own.

2. Complexity + HEAVY dropout (best dropout level, mostly p=0.4) DOES push the
   overfit ratio well below Week 18's own best dropout result in all 6 configs
   (mean ratio -85.5 pp vs Week 18 Baseline; e.g. HestonFeature Seq falls to
   2.7%, BS+NN Seq to 3.4%). But the absolute OOS RMSE guardrail rises in
   EVERY config too (mean +1.77 pp of IV vs Week 18 Baseline), and Week 19's
   best-dropout OOS RMSE is actually WORSE than Week 18's own best-dropout OOS
   RMSE in 3 of 6 configs (BS+NN Seq: 4.29% -> 5.73%; HestonFeature Seq:
   4.41% -> 5.54%; Pure NN Seq: 4.59% -> 4.85%).

VERDICT: the ratio improvement at p=0.4 is not a clean generalization win --
heavy dropout on the bigger network depresses in-sample fit at least as much
as out-of-sample error, which mechanically shrinks the ratio while absolute
predictive accuracy gets worse, not better, in half the configs. Complexity
paired with dropout is not a reliable improvement over Week 18's simpler
regularization routes (16,8 architecture or moderate dropout on the standard
architecture); if anything, Week 18's dropout sweep on the smaller network is
the better accuracy/overfitting tradeoff for most frameworks.

Next step:
Given complexity+dropout does not clearly beat Week 18's simpler regularization
routes on absolute accuracy, a natural next step is a moderate-complexity
sweep (e.g. 64->32->16, between Week 18's 32->16->8 and Week 19's
128->64->32->16) to map out where the capacity/dropout tradeoff actually
bottoms out, rather than jumping straight to the largest architecture tested
so far.
